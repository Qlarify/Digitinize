The six films this department runs on

Each earns its place. Cut to size, never inflate — a department publishing four of these steadily beats one publishing all six badly.

  • Fever: when to worry, when to wait — The single most-watched paediatrics video in any city. A paediatrician giving parents a calm rule book for the 2 a.m. decision.
  • Vaccination timeline explained — Every visit, every shot, every "why" — the schedule that parents screenshot and keep.
  • Newborn care: the first forty days — Bathing, feeding, sleeping, danger signs. The film new parents share in family groups.
  • NICU tour for anxious parents — For parents with a baby in the unit — and the ones who might need it. Calm staff, warmth, competence; no babies identifiable without consent.
  • Growth and development milestones — What a two-year-old should do, and when to seek help. Screening-friendly, share-friendly, referral-friendly.
  • Children's emergency walk-through — Where to come, what to bring, who sees your child first. Panic is a poor navigator; a familiar video is a good one.

Consent and dignity in Paediatrics video

Paediatrics ethics are simple and absolute: faces only with a parent's signed consent, many shots use hands, backs and toys, the NICU follows the unit head's rules, and distress is never staged. A doll for the newborn demo protects everyone. When a parent testimonial exists, it is the most powerful film in the set — and the rarest; one a year is plenty.

Start with the paperwork: our patient video consent kit is free and printable — signed before any recording, filed with the project record.
