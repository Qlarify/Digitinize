Resources for hospital marketing teams

Cover the event in one day, publish it in one week

A health camp, a CME, a unit launch — hospital events deserve video that lives longer than the event itself. This checklist keeps the shoot unhurried and the recap out while people still remember the day.

One week before

  • Get the brief in writing: what the event is, who opens it, what moment matters most, and what the video is for (recap for social, record for leadership, both).
  • Permissions, not apologies: the venue owner, the chief guest's office, and the unit head each confirm filming is welcome. Guest speakers especially — some decline cameras, and the day is not the time to find out.
  • The must-have shot list, named: the plaque unveiling, the chief guest's handshake, the crowd at its fullest, the department banner. Write them down; on the day nobody remembers.
  • Consent logistics for crowds: where filming is announced (a board at registration), and who walks the consent forms for any close-up.
  • Kit against the venue: power points, rain plan, a walk of the space at the same hour the event runs. Light at 9 a.m. is not light at 4 p.m.

On the day

  • Arrive one hour early. An empty venue gives you the establishing shots a full one never will.
  • Wide, medium, tight — every segment, all three. The edit lives on options, dies on single angles.
  • Catch the arrival, not the speech only. The chief guest arriving, the queue forming, the first consultation — these are the recap's heartbeat.
  • Interviews in a quiet corner, three questions each: why this matters, what happens next, one thing people should know. Ninety seconds per person, scheduled, not ambushed.
  • Numbers on camera: screenings done, patients seen, units launched. A recap film earns its facts; write them down as the day goes.
  • When the event runs late, the coverage gets smaller, not faster. Cut an angle, never cut the must-have list.

After the day

  • Same evening: footage backed up twice, take log written, the must-have list ticked against what was actually captured.
  • Within two days: first cut of the main recap — 90 seconds, the event's arc, one number, one quote.
  • Within the week: the cutdowns — a 30-second vertical, a photo-carousel of the best frames, and the best single interview as its own clip.
  • Thank the contributors: the unit head, the interviewed guests, the consented patients. The thank-you is the reason next event's permissions come easy.
  • File everything with the project record: consents, the brief, the shot log. The next event's team will start from this one.
