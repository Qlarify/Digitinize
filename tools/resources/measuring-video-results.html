Views are not results: measuring hospital video honestly

A video that brings no patient, no enquiry and no trust is a cost, whatever its view count says. This guide sets the metrics that matter, the ones that flatter, and the one-page report your leadership meeting actually wants.

Three tiers of truth

TierMetricWhat it is really telling you
FlatteryViews, likes, subscriber countReach happened. Nothing more. Report it, never lead with it.
QualityRetention at 30 s and 50%; average view duration; watch-time per uploadWhether the video held the human it reached. This is the fixable metric — read the drop-off minute.
BusinessTraced enquiries (calls to the video's number, form fills, booking-link clicks, OPD mentions of the video)Whether the hospital earned anything. This is the only tier the board funds next year on.

Tracing enquiries without lying to yourself

  • One trackable number per campaign. A phone number used only by the video's audience is the simplest, most honest attribution a hospital can run.
  • Ask the front desk. "How did you hear about us?" on the OPD registration — a checkbox, one of them being the video's name. Imperfect, free, and better than nothing.
  • Never claim full credit. A patient watched three videos, read the website, then came. Say "video-touched enquiries", not "video-generated patients". One honest phrase buys you a year of credibility.
  • Cost per traced enquiry — the number that lets you compare a health-day reel against a newspaper ad, and the one that doubles budgets when it is good.

The one-page monthly report

Ten minutes to fill, one meeting to defend. Print it, don't slide it.

RowWhat goes in it
Videos publishedCount, by format — bytes, reels, stories, events
Watch-time and retention trendUp/down vs last month, with the worst drop-off minute named
Traced enquiriesNumber, source (call / link / desk mention), by video
Top video and whyOne sentence of diagnosis, not celebration
Fix for next monthOne change, made because of the data above

The sentence that ends every good report: "Here is what we stopped doing, because the numbers said so." Killing a format is the strongest evidence a team owns its metrics.
