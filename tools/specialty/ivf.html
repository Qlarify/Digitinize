Sample shot list for one shoot day

A half-day IVF and fertility session with one consultant typically yields three to four finished videos.

  1. Fertility specialist, soft-toned consultation room: first visit explained
  2. Embryology lab: ICSI microscope b-roll, embryologist at work, no identifiers
  3. Myths segment: two consultants alternating, warm and slightly humorous
  4. Graphics: simple success-factor chart, no invented numbers, hospital's own audit only
  5. Couple testimonial if consented: faces optional, voices often enough
  6. Ultrasound room: friendly scan routine, screen out of focus
  7. B-roll: the centre's calm interiors, plants, soft light — the anti-clinic register
  8. Reception: package counselling room, privacy emphasised, booking line on screen

Questions IVF and fertility teams ask us

Is an embryology lab safe to film?

With the lab director's rules: no patient material in identifiable frame, no compromising the sterile field, staff faces with consent. The resulting film is every centre's most-watched asset.

Success-rate videos — risky?

We only use numbers your medical team approves, framed per regulatory guidance, no comparisons, no guarantees. Honest ranges outperform inflated claims with exactly the patients you want.

Will couples agree to testimonials?

Many will — with identity controls: first names, voice-only options, filmed with their own counsellor present. One story carries a page; you need not flood it.

How does video help against aggregator platforms?

Aggregators sell price. You sell trust and process — and only your own video can carry the embryologist's face saying "this is how we handle your embryos."

Languages?

English plus Kannada and Hindi minimum; fertility content carries heavy regional-language search, so a wider subtitle set earns its cost here.

Delivery?

First cut in two working days, up to three revision rounds.
