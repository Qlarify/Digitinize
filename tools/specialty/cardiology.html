What video works for cardiology

Formats we produce for cardiology departments, with the reason each one earns its place.

Heart-attack warning signs reel

Sixty seconds, vertical, a senior cardiologist naming the signs people dismiss — jaw pain, cold sweat, the "gas" feeling. This is the single most shared cardiology video a hospital makes.

Angioplasty vs bypass explainer

The question every family asks in the cath-lab waiting area. A calm two-minute comparison removes the fear that blocks consent.

Chest-pain clinic walk-through

From gate to ECG in under ten minutes, on camera. Patients who have seen the route take it instead of waiting at home.

Cardiac rehab patient story

A bypass patient back at work, walking, living. Rehab enrolment climbs every time a hospital publishes one.

Echo and cath-lab tour

Machines look frightening until a doctor explains them in 90 seconds. Bookings for non-invasive checks follow.

Heart check-up package video

What the preventive package contains, who needs it at what age, and how to book. The direct OPD driver for cardiology.

Sample shot list for one shoot day

A half-day cardiology session with one consultant typically yields three to four finished videos.

  1. Senior cardiologist, consultation room, warm light: warning signs, one topic per take
  2. Cath-lab corridor walk with the doctor narrating (staff and patient faces out of frame or consented)
  3. Angioplasty vs bypass: whiteboard or tablet graphics, doctor voice-over
  4. Patient story: home or workplace visit after recovery, family present with consent
  5. Echo room: gel, probe, screen close-up with doctor pointing at a healthy heart
  6. Rehab gym: patient on treadmill with physiotherapist, no faces without consent
  7. Front desk: package details, price on inquiry card, booking phone number on screen
  8. B-roll: waiting area, rhythm monitors, hands of a nurse adjusting an ECG clip

Questions cardiology teams ask us

Will senior consultants actually record?

The ones who agree to a fixed monthly slot, yes. We book thirty minutes around their OPD and get three to four finished videos from it — the time cost is small enough that even busy unit heads keep the slot.

Can we shoot inside the cath lab?

With the clinical team's permission and strict frame rules: no patients in the background unless consented, no screens with identifiable data, sterile field untouched. We plan the shot list with the unit before the day.

Are patient stories safe to publish?

Only with a signed consent form, taken before the shoot, with the patient told exactly where the film will appear. We keep the forms with the project file and re-check before publishing anything sensitive.

Do these videos bring OPD footfall?

Preventive-package and clinic walk-through videos carry a trackable booking number. Explainer videos build the trust that makes a patient choose your unit when the need arrives.

What languages should we subtitle?

For Bengaluru units we usually recommend English plus Kannada and Hindi at minimum; add Tamil, Telugu or Malayalam where the unit's mix calls for it. Our subtitles are checked by native speakers.

How fast do we get the first cuts?

Two working days after the shoot, with up to three revision rounds. A reel recorded on a shoot day is usually live the same week.
