What video works for oncology

Formats we produce for oncology departments, with the reason each one earns its place.

Screening-schedule explainer

Who needs which test at which age, in one calm minute. Screening videos fill camps and catch cancers early — the outcome story every oncology department wants on record.

Chemotherapy myths vs facts

A medical oncologist, unhurried, dismantling the three myths every family arrives with: hair, infection risk, "it's a last resort".

Day-care chemotherapy suite tour

Show the recliners, the nurses, the tea, the four-hour rhythm. Patients who have seen the suite arrive less frightened and miss fewer cycles.

Patient journey film

From biopsy fear to treatment to the one-year follow-up. Consented survivor stories are the most-watched videos on any oncology page.

Breast-lump clinic walk-through

From self-examination to FNAC in one film. Screening numbers move most when the route is familiar before the appointment.

Multidisciplinary tumour-board explainer

"Your case is discussed by a team, not one doctor" — trust made visible in ninety seconds.

Sample shot list for one shoot day

A half-day oncology session with one consultant typically yields three to four finished videos.

  1. Medical oncologist, quiet consulting room, no whitecoat-in-corridor look: myths vs facts
  2. Day-care suite: recliner row, nurse adjusting a drip, patient reading with consent
  3. Screening: doctor with an anatomical model or clean graphics, age-chart on screen
  4. Survivor story: home setting, family around, one year on — consent form signed before the shoot
  5. Breast clinic: female consultant explaining self-examination with a model
  6. Tumour board room: films of scans on screens (identifiers hidden), doctors in discussion
  7. Camp or awareness-day coverage: theme colours, crowd shots, quick doctor interviews
  8. B-roll: hospital garden or atrium for hope-coded cutaways, soft light throughout

Questions oncology teams ask us

How do you handle patient stories sensitively?

Consent first, always — signed before the shoot, with the patient told exactly where the film appears. We let patients choose first names only, keep diagnosis details at their comfort level, and show them the cut before publication.

Families often refuse filming. Is it worth trying?

More families agree than you expect when the ask comes from their own doctor after a good outcome. One consented survivor story a quarter is enough to anchor the page.

Can we show scans and treatment?

Scans yes, with identifiers hidden; procedures no. The emotional register — calm rooms, unhurried doctors, dignity — matters more than clinical footage.

What about clinical claims compliance?

We stay inside the language your medical director approves: no cure claims, no survival-rate promises, no comparisons. Every script goes to your review before the shoot day.

Which languages?

Oncology decisions are family decisions — we recommend English plus Kannada and Hindi, with additional languages for units serving a wider mix. Native speakers check every subtitle.

How fast are cuts delivered?

Two working days for the first cut, three revision rounds included. Awareness-day recaps publish within the week.
