What video works for neurology

Formats we produce for neurology departments, with the reason each one earns its place.

Stroke: the FAST reel

Face, Arm, Speech, Time — drilled in sixty seconds by a stroke neurologist, with the gate-to-needle message: we treat in minutes. The most time-critical video a hospital makes.

Migraine vs ordinary headache

The most-searched neurology topic in every city. A neurologist explaining when to worry converts a symptom-searcher into an OPD visit.

Epilepsy first-response film

What to do, what never to do, and that epilepsy is treatable. These films get shared by teachers and parents for years.

Stroke-unit tour: door to doctor

The thrombolysis window explained while the camera walks the route — gate, triage, CT, decision. Families who have seen it act faster.

Parkinson's and movement-disorder programme

Medicine, therapy and, where applicable, deep-brain-stimulation — patient outcomes shown with dignity. The specialty's flagship film.

Neuro-rehab patient story

A stroke survivor relearning to speak or walk, with the therapy team. Rehab referrals follow these films more than any other kind.

Sample shot list for one shoot day

A half-day neurology session with one consultant typically yields three to four finished videos.

  1. Stroke neurologist on camera: FAST signs, energetic, direct to camera
  2. Emergency route walk: gate, triage desk, CT suite, stroke bay — timed on screen
  3. Migraine clinic: consultant with a headache calendar, light and sound triggers explained
  4. Epilepsy: neurologist demonstrating recovery position with a nurse
  5. Movement-disorder clinic: tremor assessment, careful dignified framing, consent first
  6. Rehab story: physiotherapy gym, speech-therapy session, progress framed positively
  7. B-roll: EEG electrodes on a model, MRI suite corridor, therapy hands
  8. Family-carer interview if consented: what the fast response meant to them

Questions neurology teams ask us

How do we get emergency-department footage safely?

We film the route with a drill or during a quiet hour, with the emergency team's sign-off, no live patients in frame unless consented. The film is the route, rehearsed — patients cannot tell and do not care.

Is epilepsy content safe to publish?

With a neurologist's script and no filmed seizures. We show first response, not the event. These videos reduce the stigma that keeps patients from seeking care — most hospitals keep them pinned for years.

Do FAST reels actually bring stroke cases?

They bring the family, which is who decides in stroke. A city that has seen your gate-to-needle message brings the patient in an auto instead of waiting for the symptoms to pass.

What about Parkinson's and DBS content?

Deep-brain-stimulation stories are extraordinary trust-builders when the patient consents and the programme explains candidacy honestly. We follow your medical director's language rules exactly.

Languages?

English plus Kannada and Hindi minimum; FAST reels should be subtitled widest of all — they are meant for everyone, not only patients' families.

Turnaround?

First cut in two working days, three revision rounds. Urgent awareness-day items get same-week delivery.
