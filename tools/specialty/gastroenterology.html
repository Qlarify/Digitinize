What video works for gastroenterology

Formats we produce for gastroenterology departments, with the reason each one earns its place.

Sample shot list for one shoot day

A half-day gastroenterology session with one consultant typically yields three to four finished videos.

  1. Gastroenterologist in consultation room: endoscopy in 90 seconds, reassuring tone
  2. Suite tour: scope room, recovery recliners, nurse explaining the routine
  3. Hepatologist with a liver model: fatty liver, alcohol, diet — no blame language
  4. Dietitian and doctor together: the IBS plate, what to eat for two weeks
  5. Graphics or tablet sketches: oesophagus-stomach diagram, annotated
  6. Patient story if consented: someone who postponed the scope, then glad they didn't
  7. B-roll: endoscope screen (unidentifiable), water and fibre food shots
  8. OPD desk: how to book, prep-kit leaflet close-up, phone number on screen

Questions gastroenterology teams ask us

Can we show endoscopy footage?

Screen output yes — unidentifiable, no patient data on the monitor's edge. The story is comfort and speed, not the images.

Will patients really watch videos about this?

They will watch what they would never ask. These pages rank for exactly the symptoms people google privately — and the video is where they spend the longest time.

Does a prep video cut no-shows?

Hospitals that publish one report patients arrive better prepared and calmer. It is the cheapest operational win in this specialty.

How about liver-transplant and advanced-programme content?

As flagship films: programme introductions, donor-awareness messages, survivor stories. Script review with your medical team, always.

Languages?

English plus Kannada and Hindi minimum; embarrassment topics convert best in the patient's own language, so we recommend widening the subtitle set for these pages.

Delivery?

First cut in two working days, three revision rounds, same-week delivery for awareness-day items.
